About

One night. One sky. Never repeated.

You already have the date. The plate is how it stays in the room. When a guest asks what it is, you have the line.

Homer describes a cave on Ithaca with two gates. Porphyry (c. 234 to c. 305 AD) read it as a map of the cosmos: the north gate is where souls come down into life, the south gate where they go back up. He tied the north gate to Cancer and the south gate to Capricorn.

Everyone arrives by the north gate. One night, one sky, never repeated.

In 1887 the Paris Observatory started the Carte du Ciel: photograph the entire sky on glass, plate by plate. Each plate was stamped with a grid so every star could be measured against it. A grid is how you prove where something was.

Northgate Plates does that for the nights of your life. Each plate is a record of a sky, with the fact behind it on its page.

Three ways in

North Gate is for arrivals. A birth. A first year. A beginning.
The Hinge is for the turn. A lap around the Sun. The shortest day. The eclipse a child born now will be old enough to see.
South Gate is for distance, and for endings you chose. Someone under the same Moon, a few hours off.
