About

In the Odyssey, the cave of the nymphs on Ithaca has two gates. The one to the north is the way down for mortals. The one to the south is the way of the immortals.

Seventeen centuries ago the philosopher Porphyry read those lines as a map of the sky: souls arrive through the north gate and leave through the south.

In 1887 the director of the Paris Observatory called 56 astronomers to Paris to photograph the entire sky on glass. Twenty observatories took part, from Greenwich to Sydney. Over decades they exposed more than 22,000 plates. Each star was exposed three times, shifted into a tiny triangle, so a real star left three points and a flaw in the glass left one. The map was never finished.

A plate is a record that something was where it was, when it was.

Northgate Plates records the nights that mark a life. Each plate carries the fact behind it, with its source. Printed on fine art paper after you order.

Three ways in

North Gate is for arrivals and beginnings. A birth. A first year. A first home.

The Turn is for turning points. A milestone birthday. The shortest day. An eclipse you have already decided to see.

South Gate is for distance, and for endings you chose. Someone under the same Moon, a few hours off. A chapter closed on purpose.
